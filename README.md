## Assessment of the Expressive Power of BPMN and DMN
Business Process Model and Notation (BPMN) and Decision Model and Notation (DMN) complement each other by modeling different aspects of the Student Journey Matrix. BPMN represents the sequence of activities and the overall workflow of a student during their stay in DLSU, providing a visual representation of tasks, events, gateways, and end states. This makes the student lifecycle easy to understand and follow. DMN focuses on the business rules that determine how a student's status changes. Instead of using decision branches within the BPMN model, the decision logic is captured in the decision tables. These tables evaluate different inputs and conditions to determine the output. Separating the decision logic from the process improves readability and makes the business rules easier to maintain. Using BPMN and DMN together produces a clearer representation than using one or the other alone. It becomes more organized and easier to update when policies change without requiring major modifications to the process model.

Using Camunda Modeler, the BPMN Business Rule Tasks were linked to their corresponding DMN decision tables. This integration separates workflow logic from decision logic while keeping both models connected. As a result, the BPMN remains focused on the business process while the DMN centralizes and manages the decision rules.

## Challenges and Workarounds
One challenge encountered was that BPMN alone is not well suited for representing numerous decision rules. The original Student Journey Matrix contains many possible status transitions, and modeling each transition using exclusive gateways resulted in a complex and cluttered diagram. As the number of conditions increased, the BPMN became difficult to read and maintain. Another challenge was transforming the Student Journey Matrix, which is essentially a state-transition model, into a BPMN process model. While the Student Journey Matrix focuses on transitions between student statuses, BPMN is primarily designed to model business processes and workflows. Representing every status transition directly in BPMN using exclusive gateways resulted in a complex and cluttered process diagram. 

To address these challenges, the decision logic was separated from the process flow by using DMN decision tables. Instead of embedding numerous conditional branches within the BPMN, Business Rule Tasks were used to invoke the corresponding DMN decisions. This allowed the BPMN diagram to focus on the sequence of activities while the DMN tables captured the university's academic and administrative rules.

The DMN notation also has limitations. Although decision tables clearly represent business rules, they do not illustrate the sequence of activities or the overall process flow. DMN alone cannot fully represent the student lifecycle and must be used together with BPMN to provide a complete model. As a workaround, the decision logic was divided into multiple DMN tables instead of using one large decision table. This modular approach improves readability, simplifies maintenance, and allows each table to address a single business decision.

## Recommendations
One possible improvement to BPMN would be stronger support for state-based processes. Since student lifecycle management is fundamentally a state transition system, directly modeling state changes within BPMN could make such diagrams simpler and more intuitive. For DMN, better visualization features would be useful especially when the decision tables become increasingly large. In this project, the decision rules had to be divided into multiple tables to avoid a single, overly complex decision table. Better mechanisms for organizing related decisions and visualizing relationships, such as rule grouping, filtering, or collapsible sections, between them would improve readability and maintainability.
